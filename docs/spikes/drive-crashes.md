## 1. `hospital-road-ne-back`, (141, 276): front-left wheel into a 0.40 m kerb

- **Line.** The car drives west at 13.2 m/s with no braking, on `dr_Hospital_Road_12_s1_b`. That road is a one-way slip, 3.4 m wide, with one lane. The car keeps 1.96 m to 1.44 m left of the slip's centreline (`off`, x 155 to 140), so its left side is 2.6 to 3.1 m from the centre, against a half-width of 1.7 m. The offset shrinks slowly along the single 51.9 m graph edge from `HR26_ai2` (3 lanes `+++`, radius 3.19) to `HR12_b2` (1 lane, radius 1.7). That fits the AI still holding the left lane of the 3-lane approach after the fork.
- **Kerb.** The sidewalk Colmesh (`sidewalks_0_1.dae`) has a vertical face 0.37 to 0.40 m tall (0.35 to 0.44 m above the road decal) at the slip's south edge, 1.68 m from its centre, from x 131 to 136, flaring out to x 148. - **Contact.** The car's outline first overlaps kerb triangles (top +0.38 m) at t 7.45. At t 7.53 to 7.58 the lateral velocity jumps from -2.26 to -0.17 m/s and the heading turns 260° -> 271°, a push on the front-left. Damage registers in the batch from 7.40 (4.88 to 4.98 million). Terrain under the wheels is smooth (no step over 0.05 m), and the nearest building is 42 m away. The one-way signs are 0.6 m clear of the car's side, with their plates above roof height.
- **Game check.** `crash_damage`: part `covet_wheeldata_F` "Front Spindles" score 1.0, zone FL 0.048, no tyre down, `beamstate.damage` 12.8.
- **Verdict.** The front-left wheel and spindle struck a sidewalk kerb 0.40 m tall, which is higher than the 0.29 m tyre radius. The AI had run its left wheels about 0.9 m off the asphalt of a single-lane slip just after a 3-lane fork. Both defects are in the generated level: the kerb height (MapNG) and the lane count and graph at the fork (MapNG export or the BeamNG AI).

## 3. I-008: is the Lady Ishbel drop real? Yes, in OSNI; no fix applied

Probe: `pipeline/elevation_probe.py` (run 15 November on `H:\BelfastData\elevation\slice\gpxz.f32`, the raw OSNI raster `H:\BelfastData\lidar\mosaic\osni_10m.npy` and the LiDAR raster beside it).

- **The "GPXZ" file is OSNI.** The MapNG slot is called `gpxz`, but `docs/elevation.md` builds it from the Belfast LiDAR plus OSNI 10 m. At this site the source mask is 100% OSNI (4 800 of 4 800 pixels within 30 m north and south and 40 m east and west); the nearest LiDAR pixel is 817 m away. No GPXZ data exists in the build, and there is no second height source here to compare against.
- **No pipeline artefact.** Along y = -146 (x -215 to -181), file minus raw OSNI (bilinear) is 0.54 m everywhere (min = max), which is the area's seam offset. So the shape is OSNI's, and so is the drop: 3.37 m in 12 m (x -202 to -190) in the file, 3.37 m in the raw raster.
- **The raw cells show a landform, not a blunder.** In the 10 m grid around (-196, -146) a plateau at 47.2 to 47.6 m (flat to 0.2 m over 60 m) ends at an edge between two adjacent cells (47.3 to 44.1 m, a 3.2 m step in 10 m), then falls 2.2 to 2.7 m per cell (about 22 to 27%) eastwards for 60 m. The edge repeats in all 15 rows of the 150 m block (about 75 m north to 75 m south of the site), moving about 30 m west going south, so it is a continuous escarpment more than 140 m long. A cut along the edge (x -196, y -176 to -116) rises only 3.0 m in 60 m (5%), and a cut across it (-215, -80 to -190, -240) drops 1.4 m in 12 m only where it crosses the edge. The OSM cut puts a wood 21 m away and Purdy's Burn 104 m away, which fits the side of a stream valley. OSNI's 10 m model is bare earth (interpolated from the 2014 to 2016 enhanced DTM); `docs/elevation.md` puts the OSNI-to-LiDAR scatter at -0.6 to +1.4 m (10th to 90th percentile), well under 3.3 m.
- **Verdict: real as far as the open data can say.** Not confirmed by a second source, because the 2006 LiDAR does not reach here and the 30 m Terrarium file (`t30.f32`) is too smooth to show it (it sits 2.2 m above the height file on the plateau and 12 m above it in the valley). It is not refuted either. It is not an artefact of our pipeline, so there is nothing for an override to flatten, and an override would need evidence that OSNI is wrong.
- **What MapNG built (i005 zip).** The road already spreads the 3.3 m drop: z 38.9 m at x -214.6 falls at 15% (0.15 m per metre) to 36.3 m at x -194.6, then eases to 7.5% (35.66 m at the junction, x -188.6). The sag is a grade change of about 7.5% in 4 to 6 m, a vertical-curve radius near 60 m. On a front overhang of 1.9 m that is about 3 cm of ground rise (1.9^2 / (2 x 60)), so the geometry alone does not explain a scrape without the braking dive; the cause stays inferred, not measured.
- **Why no slope limit.** Limiting the graded slope at the junction mouth means changing how MapNG grades the road (a patch, an Opus task) or lowering the real terrain in the height file. Neither is justified: the scrape no longer fails the drive gate (it is logged as `scrape`, `lady-ishbel-loop-reverse` completes on the i005 build, 15 November 00:27 night run), the damage reading shows no damaged part, and the 15% ramp matches what OSNI says is there. If the user wants a gentler mouth anyway, the row to raise is "MapNG: vertical-curve easing at a junction mouth, radius at least 150 m".

## 4. I-007: the AI kept the approach's outer lane onto the slip; MapNG patch 0007

- **Cause.** `Hospital_Road_26` (OSM way 1410878962: one-way, lanes 3, `turn:lanes` through|right|right) forks at (150, 280) into `Hospital_Road_12` (way 860893160, 1 lane, 3.4 m) and `road_33688116` (2 lanes, 6 m). All three start on the fork node, the approach's centre line. In the i005 navgraph the fork node is HR26's `_ai` end (radius 4.875), HR12's start (1.7) and road_33688116's start (3.0) merged, radius 3.19 (the mean, `map.lua:1280-1281`). The next HR12 node is 51.9 m on: optimizeNodes drops the nodes of a straight road whose radius does not change (`map.lua:1913-1964`). At the fork the AI keeps the incoming `+++` (the narrower range, `ai.lua:1680-1686`) and lane 1 (left-hand traffic bias, `ai.lua:1377`). That is 2/3 of the node's half width, (4.875 + 1.7) / 2, so 2.19 m left. From there it eases to the slip centre over the 52 m edge. Telemetry, run `2026-11-15_002703`: car body centre 2.19 m left at the fork, 1.79 m left 10 m past it. The lane count is right (3 = 1 + 2), so it stays.
- **Fix (patch 0007, `services/laneForkLeadIns.js`, hooked into `exportBeamNGLevel.js`).** A lane fork is one one-way way in, two or more one-way ways out, and the lanes add up. A branch whose lanes' centre in the approach lies more than (its half width - 0.9 m) off its own line gets its AI path in two pieces. The first, `_lead_ai`, runs from the fork, 0.15 m wider each side, to the first node at least max(6 m, the approach's half width + 1 m) along whose distance from every other branch's line is more than its half width + 0.25 m. The second, `_ai`, is the rest. The lead-in's end survives for three reasons. It is a decal end node. The 0.15 m radius step keeps it out of the end-node merge, which needs a difference under 0.1 m (`map.lua:1224`). In the last optimize its radius, 1.78, is far off the line from the fork node's 3.24. A first try with one `_ai` tapering in width failed: a changing width makes map.lua thin the decal's subdivisions by position only (`map.lua:645-717`), so the node went. The graph still had a 52.6 m edge, and the body centre was 1.94 m left at 10 m (run `2026-11-15_164659`, build kept in `H:\BelfastData\mapng\i007\attempt1_out`).
- **Result.** Build `H:\BelfastData\mapng\i007\out\belfast_slice.zip`, in the QA profile's `mods`. Graph: fork -> `dr_Hospital_Road_12_s1_lead_ai2` (140.26, 277.88), 10.0 m, radius 1.78. - **Static checks** (`H:\BelfastData\mapng\i007\static`). The terrain is unchanged (`terrain.ter` CRC equal to i005's).
